# Style contract

These are non-negotiable and they go into **every** generation, verbatim in spirit,
without being softened to make a hard prompt pass. If a clip needs one of these broken,
that is a decision the student makes out loud, not something the model negotiates away.

## The twelve

1. **iPhone 12 realism.** Use the literal phrase. Ordinary phone video, not a production.
2. **Fine natural grain.** The frame must not look perfectly clean.
3. **Natural daylight**, and always state where the light comes from. No studio, no ring light.
4. **NO background blur, NO bokeh, NOT cinematic.** Deep focus. No HDR, no colour grading.
5. **Realistic physics.** Weight, materials, how things fall, how fabric moves.
6. **No slow motion.** Real time. Not sped up either.
7. **Ultra smooth animation.** No stutter, no morphing.
8. **Vivid but natural colour.** Eye appealing, never oversaturated AI colour.
9. **Camera is static** unless the clip explicitly says otherwise. The exception is handheld
   micro shake on selfie and discovery hooks, where the format calls for it.
10. **Ultra natural voices.** Describe the voice as a human register (who is talking, to whom,
    with what energy), never as a setting. This is what stops it sounding like AI.
11. **9:16 vertical** is the default for everything.
12. **Product is 1:1 with the reference.** Never modified, never morphed, never recoloured.

## And one rule about what does not belong in the generation

**No music and no text overlay inside the generated clip.** Sound design yes. Music and text
arrive later, in the edit and in TikTok. A clip that comes back with burned in text is a reroll.

## Why this file exists rather than a pasted master prompt

Adam's master prompt works because it is **identical every single time**, not because of its
wording. A file that gets imported into every session is the same thing with the variance
removed: nothing to forget, nothing to paraphrase, nothing lost when the chat gets long.
